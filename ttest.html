Does Your Writing Hand Beat Your Other Hand?

What We Did

For our class experiment, we set out to determine whether individuals could write the phrase “I am amazing” more times using their dominant hand compared to their non-dominant hand. We selected nine (9) students and compared their results against a benchmark figure, bringing our total to 10 results. During the experiment, each participant took a turn writing the phrase as many times as possible within 30 seconds using each hand. We also included one “Yes/No” question: “Are you able to write in cursive?” This question was included to examine whether the ability to write in cursive had an influence on the participants’ writing speed.

The Results

The results show a positive direction in favor of the dominant hand. All nine students wrote the phrase “I am amazing” more times using their dominant hand compared to their non-dominant hand within the 30-second time frame. The average number of phrases written was higher for the dominant hand (6.33) compared to the non-dominant hand (2.56), representing a mean difference of 3.78 phrases. Furthermore, the results were statistically significant, with a p-value of less than 0.001, indicating a significant difference in writing performance between the two hands.

In Plain Words

In our experiment, we wanted to find out whether students could write the phrase “I am amazing” faster using their dominant hand compared to their non-dominant hand. Each student had 30 seconds to write the phrase as many times as possible with each hand, and we also asked whether they could write in cursive to see if it affected their speed. The results showed that all nine students wrote an average of approximately 3 more phrases using their dominant hand compared to their non-dominant hand, and the difference was statistically significant.

What I Would Do Differently

One limitation of the experiment was the small sample size of only nine students, which may limit the generalizability of the results to a larger population. To improve the experiment, we could have included a larger and more diverse group of participants to obtain more reliable results and better represent the general population.
