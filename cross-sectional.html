Cross-Sectional Study

Understanding what repeated cross-sectional surveys can—and cannot—tell us about anxiety trends among young adults.

Research methods & interpretation
Research interpretation

The researchers’ conclusion

Original claim

“As young adults have moved through their twenties over the past decade, their anxiety has steadily worsened – a generation is becoming more anxious as it ages.”

How the survey was conducted

In the study, researchers conducted a national household survey on mental health from 2014 to 2023. Each year, they randomly selected a new sample of approximately 27,000 adults and deliberately ensured that participants were not surveyed more than once. As a result, the samples remained independent from year to year.

8.1%Adults aged 18–25 reporting frequent anxiety symptoms in 2014
14.7%Adults aged 18–25 reporting frequent anxiety symptoms in 2023

Limitations of cross-sectional data

The researchers’ claim assumes that the same generation is becoming more anxious as its members grow older. However, the study does not follow the same individuals over time. It was specifically designed not to select the same person twice, so the people surveyed in 2014 were not necessarily the same people surveyed in 2023.

This illustrates one of the limitations of cross-sectional data. It measures different people at particular points in time, allowing researchers to describe differences or similarities between groups and identify trends within a population over time. However, it cannot determine or measure changes within specific individuals because those individuals are not being followed.

A more accurate conclusion

What the findings actually show

“The percentage of adults aged 18–25 experiencing frequent anxiety symptoms increased from 8.1% in 2014 to 14.7% in 2023, indicating a population-level increase in reported anxiety symptoms.”

This interpretation recognizes the trend without assuming that the same individuals experienced increasing anxiety as they aged.
