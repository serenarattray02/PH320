Cross Sectional Study

National health statistics center · published annually

Each year since 2014, the center has conducted a national household survey on mental health. Every year a new random sample of roughly 27,000 adults is drawn; no one is deliberately surveyed twice, and the samples are independent from year to year. Each respondent completes the interview once, answering questions about symptoms in the past thirty days.

The share of adults aged 18 to 25 reporting frequent anxiety symptoms rose from 8.1% in 2014 to 14.7% in 2023. Over the same period, the share among adults aged 50 to 64 was roughly flat. The center publishes the figures with confidence intervals and notes that the survey mode shifted from in-person to a mix of phone and web interviewing in 2019.

What the authors concluded

“As young adults have moved through their twenties over the past decade, their anxiety has steadily worsened — a generation is becoming more anxious as it ages.”
